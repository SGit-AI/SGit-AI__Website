Agents and Incidents Day

A one-day, workshop-only event in central London, run the way the Open Security Summit was run: no presentations, every table producing something, everything released under Creative Commons. RiskMandate brings the agent behaviour policies. DECID:R brings the incident. The summit brings the method. Every participant leaves with a policy for an agent they actually run, a debrief from an incident they actually decided, and an encrypted vault holding both.

not public · a page for the three organisers to argue with · figures are proposals, not prices

Why this, why the three of us

The Open Security Summit ran from 2018 at Woburn Forest Center Parcs as a five-day working event, organised by Dinis Cruz and Jemma Davis. Its own description still holds: "This is not a conference with uni-directional presentations." Every session was a working session aimed at a specific problem, and the 2018 summit alone, with more than two hundred participants, produced outcomes that are still in use, among them the Mobile Security Testing Guide work, the OWASP threat modelling pages and the Application Security Guide for CISOs. The thing people remember is not a talk. It is that they made something with strangers in a room and it was published the same week.

The two problems this day puts on the tables are the two that the same community is stuck on in 2026, and they are the same problem seen from two ends. RiskMandate's question is what can your agent do, and what did you actually ask it to do: reach, mandate, the gap between them, and the barriers that stand in the gap, typed honestly as a boundary, a setting, an expectation or nothing. DECID:R's question is when it goes wrong, what do you decide, and what does each decision cost: six decision points under time pressure, with public trust, operational capacity, threat containment and financial cost tracked live, and a debrief and runbook at the end. Put together, a table can write the policy for an agent, run the incident that agent could cause, and see how different the incident is when the policy exists. That is the day.

Nothing here is a product launch. Everything a table produces, scenario, policy, decisions, debrief, is released under CC BY, as the summit did. The participants are the authors.

The shape of the day

Full day, one venue, six tables. Three rounds of ninety minutes, each table a different agent and a different audience, participants choosing a table per round. Each round runs the DECID:R scenario twice: once for an organisation with no behaviour policy, and once with the policy the table has just written. The facilitator keeps the two decision traces and the two sets of metrics side by side for the readout.

The two-run rule

Every table runs its incident twice, and the order matters. First, the organisation with no policy: the agent has the reach the connector gives it and nothing written down, and the table decides the six points as that organisation would. Then the table writes the policy, in RiskMandate's four parts, for that same agent, and runs the incident again as the organisation that has it. The facilitator keeps both traces. The readout is six pairs of traces, and the question for the room is what the policy changed: which decisions it removed, which it made cheaper, and which it made no difference to at all. That last category is the honest one, and the summit's record is that honest outcomes are the ones that get reused.

What each partner puts in, and what a seat is worth

RiskMandate · Dinis

A policy per participant, and a vault

Each participant leaves with an Agent Behaviour Policy for an agent they run, corrected at the table and signed off by a facilitator, in an encrypted vault they hold the key to. On the published ladder that sits between the £500 corrected tier and the £1,500 reviewed-and-signed tier; a facilitator signs it in the room but there is no second session afterwards. Proposed declared value: £750 per seat.

DECID:R · Jemma

A facilitated exercise, debrief and runbook

The table's incident, run live on the platform, with the un-watermarked debrief pack and the generated runbook taken home. DECID:R prices per organisation; its Gold tier puts two senior facilitators in a room for a day with a bespoke scenario. Jemma to put the number on it. If it is also £750, the seat carries a clean £1,500 declared value, which is a sentence that sells itself.

Operational plan

Ten weeks from the day this page is agreed. The first two weeks cost nothing but conversations; the date is not fixed until the venue is.

Week 0Agree the plan. Dinis and Jemma: name, full day or half, the seat value on the DECID:R side, the split, the two candidate dates. Decide the licence line once: CC BY 4.0 on every outcome.
Week 1Open the first thirty. Publish the page on both sites. Registration writes to the event vault's append lane; no form service, no mailing list, the vault is the list. Announce to the summit community, the RiskMandate early-access members and the DECID:R customer base.
Weeks 1 to 3Find the venue and the headline sponsor. Two asks, run in parallel, each with a one-page version of this plan. The venue sponsor is the easier ask: a company with a floor and a reason to be seen hosting this. The date is fixed the day the venue says yes.
Weeks 2 to 5Recruit six facilitators and six scribes. From the summit's track leaders and the two communities. One facilitator briefing call, one written brief per table (scenario, policy worksheet, the two-run rule, the readout format). Scribes write the outcomes into the table's vault as the day runs.
Weeks 3 to 6Write the six scenarios. Each in DECID:R's shape (six decision points, three options each) and each with a matching policy worksheet. Dinis drafts the agent and the policy side; Jemma drafts the incident side; one rehearsal per table, remote, with the facilitator.
Weeks 4 to 8Sell seats 31 to 80 and the supporter slots. Through the event-organiser partners on the split, and directly. Price set at week 4 from the first-thirty take-up.
Week 8Provision. One vault per registered participant, pre-seeded with the policy worksheet for their chosen table and the day's agenda; a read key sent the week before, the write key handed over at the door. Print the paper tables' decision cards and worksheets.
Week 9Dry run. Table 1 and table 6 run end to end with the organisers as participants, including the readout and the publishing step. Fix what breaks.
Week 10The day. Publish the outcomes vault before the close. Within a week: a write-up on each of the three sites, the six before-and-after pairs as the headline, and the question of whether to run it again.

Roles on the day

Two hosts

Dinis and Jemma run the open and the readout, standing, and float between tables. Neither facilitates a table.

Six facilitators

One per table, from the community. Runs the policy worksheet, then the two DECID:R runs, then prepares the table's two-minute readout.

Six scribes

One per table. Writes the policy, the decisions and the debrief into the table's vault as they happen, so publishing at 15:30 is a push, not a write-up.

One operator

Keeps the shared display, the vaults and the append lane working. Hands out write keys at the door. Has the printed fallbacks.

Things that go wrong, and what we do

  • No headline sponsor by week 4. Run it anyway at sixty seats with the venue sponsor and supporters, and price seats 31 to 60 at the top of the range. The first thirty stay free; that promise is the reputation.
  • A table has no participants. Collapse to five tables at the open; the facilitator joins the board table, which always fills.
  • Wi-Fi fails. Every laptop table has the paper version of its scenario and worksheet. DECID:R's display is the only thing that needs the network, and the operator has the scenarios exported.
  • Someone brings a real incident. Good. The board table takes it after the readout, unrecorded, and nothing from it is published.

Decisions the two of you need to make

  1. Full day or half day. The memo leans full day, and the two-run rule needs ninety-minute rounds, so this plan is a full day. A half day would be two rounds and no board table. Say which.
  2. The name. Working title here is Agents and Incidents Day. Alternatives on the table: Control and Respond, The Gap Day, or simply Open Security Summit London, agents edition, which borrows the pedigree directly.
  3. The DECID:R seat value, and whether the Gold-tier two-facilitators-in-the-room is what Jemma brings, or the platform with community facilitators.
  4. The split between the three parties on supporter money and paid seats, agreed before the first seat sells.
  5. Two candidate dates. Ten weeks from a week-0 decision in early October lands in mid-December; the honest alternative is the second half of January. Pick both and let the venue decide.
  6. Who writes to whom first. The venue ask and the headline ask should go out from the person with the warmer relationship, with this page attached.

What the page becomes when it goes public

This draft is for the three organisers. The public version is shorter and has three jobs: say what the day is in one screen (the first picture above), let someone take one of the first thirty seats (a form that writes to the append lane, no account), and let a sponsor see the three tiers and write to one address. It goes on riskmandate.ai and on sgit.ai as the same page, and on decidr.live if Jemma wants it there, with the registration lane shared between them so there is one list.

Every number on this page is a proposal. The ones that will survive are the ones the first thirty registrations and the first sponsor conversation confirm.
