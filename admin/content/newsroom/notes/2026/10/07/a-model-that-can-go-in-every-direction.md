!quote how-much-of-this-did-i-write | A model that can go in every direction needs someone with a direction.

It sits in a bullet halfway down an article whose job is to count words: how many went in, how many came out, how many rounds each piece took. It is the idea the author picked as the article's strongest, when asking for its infographic to lead with one, and it is worth reading on its own, because it is the answer to a question the site asks in other places without saying so.

The same article gives the evidence for it. The corrections over four weeks were almost never facts:

!quote how-much-of-this-did-i-write | The rest are a model choosing a reasonable direction from several, and me saying which one I meant.

And it draws the consequence for people whose job looks threatened by the output:

!quote how-much-of-this-did-i-write | What the loop needs from a person is the thing the model does not have: the direction, the unique angle, the taste, the twenty years, the knowledge of which of the reasonable guesses is the right one.

That is the same claim [the code review company article](/articles/if-somebody-built-a-company-on-code-review.html) makes about architects and reviewers, and the same place [the Mandate Stack](/articles/the-mandate-stack.html) puts its one human: not doing the work, choosing among it. The collection [What the human brings](/articles/collections/what-the-human-brings.html) puts the four articles that make this argument side by side.
