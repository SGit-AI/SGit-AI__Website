Two days ago the desk pulled out a sentence about direction:

!quote how-much-of-this-did-i-write | A model that can go in every direction needs someone with a direction.

On 8 October four articles reached the other half of that idea. The first says it plainly:

!quote knowing-when-to-stop | An entity that can go anywhere has no natural reason to stop anywhere.

The second shows what happens when the stop is added as more instructions, one rule per mistake, until the rules are the problem:

!quote every-mistake-added-a-rule | If a session needs 100 KB of rules to be productive, the rules are doing the job that structure should do.

The third puts the rule in a different place, outside the model:

!quote agency-is-not-a-yes | Models prepare and propose; rules decide; people own the rules.

And the fourth measures it, in one customer service agent built three ways:

!quote hope-or-enforcement | Every design has a policy for every behaviour. The difference is who enforces it.

Read in order, they are one argument. Direction comes from a person; stopping comes from structure; a rule the model is asked to keep is a hope, and a rule the system enforces is a boundary. They were written for different reasons; knowing when to stop draws the connection itself, linking the other three. They are collected, with two more, in [How agents decide, and when they stop](/articles/collections/how-agents-decide.html).
