The story vault was argued in September as a different shape for news: the reporting kept as a graph of claims and evidence, the article as one projection of it. In two days of October it met a practical test, and the test was local.

[Story vault underneath, Reader Skills on top](/articles/story-vault-meets-reader-skills.html) takes Markus Franz's Reader Skills and finds that each one is an operation the story graph already supports. [The bridge, followed to the end](/articles/the-bridge-followed-to-the-end.html) plays his bridge example out as a simulation, with a published vault: a story newsworthy on five days and needed on fifty-seven. Its economics were corrected the next day, and the correction is recorded on the site.

[The waiting room knew first](/articles/the-waiting-room-knew-first.html) is the same idea on a live story: a hospital IT outage that only the people already in the waiting room knew about, with the evidence kept in a vault while the story was still moving. [Liquid content needs water](/articles/liquid-content-needs-water.html) answers FT Strategies: what makes content liquid is the reporting inside it, so liquefy the journalist's notebook, not the finished article.

The two September pieces underneath are [the future of news is a story vault, not a paywall](/articles/future-of-news-story-vault-not-paywall.html) and [how news got here](/articles/how-news-got-here.html).
