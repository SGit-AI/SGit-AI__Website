Reading one map

A map is a way to tell a story. This deck reads one out, frame by frame: an afternoon on sgit.ai in which a reading meter, pay by depth, personas and an encrypted share shipped, drawn one release at a time.

Each slide asks one question of the frame. How do I think about this, what am I seeing, where do I focus.

2 / 10

Before: where is the user, and what exists?

Where is the user? At the top: the reader, on every device.

What exists? Everything on the right: the articles and the build, git, browser storage, vaults, the sealed envelope, Stripe. Nothing on the left.

What am I seeing? A business whose user only touches mature things. Comfortable, and not a strategy.

3 / 10

A meter: is anything above the new dot?

The first new dot appears at genesis, close to the user, standing on browser storage.

The question: is anything above it? Yes, the user, directly.

Where to focus: this is the right altitude. A new thing the user touches is worth more than a better thing three layers down.

4 / 10

Live: what was built, and what was bought?

Pay by depth, below zero with no block, a Stripe link. Three new dots.

Look at the edges. Every one runs to the right. Nothing new was built below the line.

What am I seeing? The build-or-buy reading, clean: custom at the top, commodity underneath.

5 / 10

Personas: where did we hook in?

The dot that matters is not the persona. It is the edge: the persona connects to the articles, the oldest and largest thing the site has.

Where to hook was answered by the most mature component on the map.

Where to focus: a new feature that stands on the thing you already have most of is cheap and hard to copy.

6 / 10

Versions: why was this one free?

A small dot, and it stands on git.

It cost almost nothing because its shoulder was a commodity that the site already stood on.

What am I seeing? The frame I use to explain why some features are free and some are not: look at what is under them.

7 / 10

The encrypted share: is the chain ours?

The first dot that stands on a vault.

What am I seeing? The custom thing we had to build sits on the one product we made ourselves, so the chain under it is ours end to end.

Where to focus: this is where the map starts to pay for itself. Everything above the vault is now a reason for the vault.

8 / 10

The reader card: did the user notice?

Usefulness sets the price. Nothing new under it; it is a change at the top of the chain, where the user is.

The flow reading: everything below now has a reason to exist that the user can see.

What moved the needle? This one. It is the smallest dot on the map and the only one the reader would name.

9 / 10

Next: which dot moves first?

Dashed dots: real payments, a persona in your own vault, other sites.

The story's last line is always the same question: which of these moves first, and what does it stand on?

Where did we walk back? Not on this map; the two walk-backs of the following days are on the leverage map in the article.

10 / 10

Seven questions, one picture

  • Where is the user?
  • What is new?
  • What does it stand on?
  • Where did the movement happen?
  • Where did we walk back?
  • Where would somebody else hook in?
  • What is next?

A reader who did not know the business now knows what it is. That is what a map is for.
